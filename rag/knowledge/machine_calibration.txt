MACHINE CALIBRATION AND PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE
================================================

1. WHY CALIBRATION MATTERS
----------------------------
Calibration ensures that machine sensors, controllers, and actuators produce
accurate and repeatable results. Uncalibrated machines produce parts that drift
out of specification even when process parameters appear to be within range.

Calibration must be performed:
- On a scheduled basis (see intervals below).
- After any repair, component replacement, or major maintenance.
- When process quality unexpectedly deteriorates.
- After any machine relocation.

2. CALIBRATION INTERVALS (GENERAL GUIDELINES)
-----------------------------------------------
Component                    Calibration Interval
---------------------------  --------------------
Temperature sensors          Every 3 months
Pressure transducers         Every 6 months
Vibration sensors            Every 6 months
Speed / tachometer           Every 6 months
Load cells / force gauges    Every 3 months
Position / dimensional gauges  Every month
Overall machine accuracy     Every 12 months (or after major overhaul)

3. CALIBRATION PROCEDURE (TEMPERATURE EXAMPLE)
------------------------------------------------
1. Allow machine to reach thermal equilibrium (30 min warm-up).
2. Place a calibrated reference thermometer at the same location as the sensor.
3. Record sensor reading and reference reading at three temperatures:
   - Lower operating limit (e.g., 75 °C)
   - Mid-range (e.g., 85 °C)
   - Upper operating limit (e.g., 95 °C)
4. If deviation > ±2 °C, adjust the sensor offset in the controller.
5. Re-test and document the calibration result.
6. Attach calibration label with date and next due date.

4. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE
------------------------------------
Daily:
- Visual inspection of machine for leaks, damage, or unusual noise.
- Check lubrication levels (oil, grease).
- Clean machine surfaces and remove debris.
- Verify all safety guards are in place.

Weekly:
- Check belt tension and condition.
- Inspect hoses and fittings for wear.
- Verify sensor readings match expected values.
- Check and tighten any loose fasteners.

Monthly:
- Lubricate all bearing points.
- Check alignment of drive components.
- Clean coolant system and check coolant concentration.
- Inspect tooling wear and replace if worn beyond limit.
- Test emergency stop and safety circuits.

Quarterly:
- Calibrate temperature sensors.
- Perform full vibration analysis.
- Inspect and clean heat exchangers.
- Review production quality data trends.

Annually:
- Full machine overhaul and accuracy check.
- Replace all wear parts (belts, seals, filters) proactively.
- Verify all safety systems and interlock functions.

5. CALIBRATION RECORDS
------------------------
Always maintain a written calibration record including:
- Machine ID and location.
- Date of calibration.
- Parameter calibrated.
- Before and after readings.
- Technician name and signature.
- Next calibration due date.

Poor calibration records are a common finding in quality audits (ISO 9001, IATF 16949).
