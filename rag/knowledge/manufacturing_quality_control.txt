MANUFACTURING QUALITY CONTROL - KNOWLEDGE BASE
================================================

1. OVERVIEW OF MANUFACTURING QUALITY CONTROL
---------------------------------------------
Manufacturing quality control (QC) is the process of ensuring that products are
manufactured within defined tolerances and meet customer specifications. QC in
manufacturing involves monitoring process parameters continuously to detect
deviations before defects occur.

Key principles:
- Prevention is better than correction: detect problems early using sensors.
- Statistical Process Control (SPC): use data to distinguish normal variation
  from abnormal variation.
- Root cause analysis: identify WHY a parameter is out of range, not just THAT
  it is out of range.
- Corrective and preventive actions (CAPA): document, fix, and prevent recurrence.

2. CRITICAL PROCESS PARAMETERS
--------------------------------
In most manufacturing processes, the following parameters are monitored:

a) Temperature
   - Affects material properties (hardness, viscosity, tensile strength).
   - Normal range depends on material and process (e.g., 75–95 °C for plastics).
   - High temperature can cause thermal degradation, warping, or burn marks.
   - Low temperature can cause poor bonding, brittleness, or incomplete curing.

b) Pressure
   - Drives material flow in injection molding, stamping, and extrusion.
   - Normal range: 4.5–5.5 bar for most light manufacturing processes.
   - High pressure causes flashing, cracking, or tooling wear.
   - Low pressure results in incomplete fill, voids, or weak parts.

c) Vibration
   - Caused by rotating parts, bearings, gears, or unbalanced loads.
   - Normal vibration: 0.5–1.5 mm/s RMS.
   - Elevated vibration (>2.0 mm/s) indicates bearing wear, misalignment,
     or imbalance, which degrades surface finish and dimensional accuracy.
   - Critical vibration (>3.5 mm/s) can cause tool breakage or machine damage.

d) Machine Speed (RPM)
   - Affects material processing rate, heat generation, and surface finish.
   - Normal operating range: 950–1050 RPM.
   - Over-speed causes excessive heat, vibration, and tool wear.
   - Under-speed reduces productivity and may cause material solidification.

e) Production Rate
   - Units produced per minute; derived from machine speed and process efficiency.
   - Normal rate: 45–55 units/min.
   - Drop in production rate below 40 units/min signals a problem.
   - Excessive rate above 60 units/min usually indicates skipped quality checks.

3. QUALITY STATUS DEFINITIONS
-------------------------------
- Normal:   All parameters within acceptable range. Process stable.
- Warning:  One or more parameters slightly outside range. Monitor closely.
             Intervention may be needed within 30 minutes.
- Critical: One or more parameters significantly outside range. Immediate
             intervention required to prevent defective production or machine damage.

4. DEFECT TYPES LINKED TO PROCESS PARAMETERS
---------------------------------------------
- Dimensional defects: caused by temperature fluctuation, worn tooling,
  machine speed variation.
- Surface finish defects: caused by vibration, contamination, speed instability.
- Structural defects (voids, cracks): caused by pressure anomalies.
- Burn marks: caused by high temperature or high speed generating heat.
- Incomplete fill / short shots: caused by low pressure or low temperature.

5. GENERAL CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
-------------------------------
Step 1: Stop or slow the machine if Critical status is detected.
Step 2: Identify which parameter is out of range (temperature, pressure, vibration).
Step 3: Apply the parameter-specific corrective action (see other documents).
Step 4: Run a small test batch and inspect parts before resuming full production.
Step 5: Log the event in the maintenance record.
Step 6: Update preventive maintenance schedule if wear is detected.
