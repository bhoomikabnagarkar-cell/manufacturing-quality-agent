PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE FOR MANUFACTURING QUALITY
=================================================

1. OVERVIEW
------------
Preventive Maintenance (PM) is scheduled maintenance performed to prevent
equipment failure before it occurs. PM is a cornerstone of quality manufacturing
because machine degradation directly causes product defects.

PM differs from reactive maintenance (fixing after breakdown) in that:
- PM reduces unplanned downtime by 30–50%.
- PM reduces defect rates by catching wear before it causes quality issues.
- PM is less expensive than emergency repairs.

2. KEY PM ACTIVITIES RELEVANT TO PROCESS QUALITY
--------------------------------------------------
a) Lubrication Management
   - Use the correct lubricant grade specified by the machine manufacturer.
   - Over-lubrication can attract contaminants; under-lubrication causes wear.
   - Check oil level daily; change oil on schedule (typically every 500–1000 hr).
   - Grease bearings per schedule; use a grease gun, do not over-pack.

b) Cooling System Maintenance
   - Flush and replace coolant every 6 months or per manufacturer schedule.
   - Inspect coolant hoses for cracking, swelling, or leaks monthly.
   - Clean heat exchangers and radiators quarterly.
   - Maintain coolant concentration for corrosion protection.

c) Drive System Maintenance
   - Inspect drive belts for cracking, fraying, or glazing monthly.
   - Check belt tension with a belt tension gauge; adjust if slack or over-tight.
   - Inspect chain drives for wear and lubrication.
   - Inspect and align couplings after any motor or gearbox service.

d) Tooling Management
   - Track tool life in hours and replace proactively at end of life.
   - Inspect cutting tools for chipping under magnification.
   - Re-grind or replace tools that show wear beyond defined limits.
   - Use correct cutting parameters (speed, feed, depth) to maximize tool life.

e) Seals and Gaskets
   - Inspect hydraulic and pneumatic seals for leakage monthly.
   - Replace seals at first sign of weeping or leakage.
   - Use manufacturer-specified seal material for compatibility with fluids.

3. PM SCHEDULING BEST PRACTICES
---------------------------------
- Use a Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) or simple spreadsheet.
- Assign PM tasks to specific technicians with deadlines.
- Never skip PM tasks due to production pressure — this creates larger problems.
- Perform PM during planned downtime (nights, weekends) to minimize disruption.
- After PM, always verify machine performance with a test run before production.

4. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN PM AND PROCESS PARAMETERS
---------------------------------------------------
Well-maintained machine:
  Temperature stable within ±2 °C of setpoint.
  Pressure stable within ±0.2 bar of setpoint.
  Vibration below 1.5 mm/s.
  Machine speed stable within ±20 RPM.

Under-maintained machine:
  Temperature fluctuates due to worn heater elements or blocked coolant.
  Pressure drops due to leaking seals or worn pump.
  Vibration increases due to bearing wear.
  Speed varies due to worn belts or encoder issues.

5. SIGNS THAT PM IS OVERDUE
-----------------------------
- Process parameters drifting gradually over days or weeks.
- Increasing defect rate without apparent cause.
- Unusual noise from machine (knocking, squealing, grinding).
- Increased energy consumption.
- Coolant leaks or oil stains under machine.
- Vibration trend increasing week over week.
