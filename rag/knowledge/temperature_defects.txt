TEMPERATURE-RELATED DEFECTS AND CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
====================================================

1. HIGH TEMPERATURE DEFECTS (Temperature > 95 °C)
--------------------------------------------------
Causes:
- Cooling system failure or blocked coolant channel.
- Heater controller malfunction (stuck ON).
- Excessive friction due to worn bearings or lubrication failure.
- High ambient temperature combined with inadequate cooling.

Resulting defects:
- Thermal degradation: material discoloration, brittleness, reduced tensile strength.
- Warping or dimensional distortion due to thermal expansion.
- Burn marks on product surface.
- Adhesion failure in bonded assemblies.
- Reduced tool life due to thermal fatigue.

Corrective actions for HIGH temperature:
1. Immediately reduce heater setpoint by 5–10 °C.
2. Check coolant flow rate and temperature; clean blocked coolant channels.
3. Inspect and lubricate bearings; replace if worn.
4. Verify temperature sensor calibration (may be reading incorrectly).
5. Increase cooling dwell time by 10–15%.
6. Reduce machine speed by 5–10% to reduce frictional heat.
7. Inspect material: high-moisture raw material increases processing heat.

2. LOW TEMPERATURE DEFECTS (Temperature < 75 °C)
-------------------------------------------------
Causes:
- Heater failure or controller set too low.
- Cold startup without adequate warm-up time.
- Coolant over-circulation.

Resulting defects:
- Incomplete fill (short shots) in molded parts.
- Poor bonding or weld strength.
- Brittleness and cracking in finished product.
- Increased cycle time as material flows slowly.

Corrective actions for LOW temperature:
1. Increase heater setpoint by 5–10 °C.
2. Allow machine to warm up fully (minimum 15 minutes) before production.
3. Reduce coolant flow slightly.
4. Check heater element continuity; replace faulty heaters.

3. TEMPERATURE MONITORING BEST PRACTICES
------------------------------------------
- Calibrate temperature sensors every 3 months.
- Use redundant sensors at critical points.
- Set alarms at 90% of the upper limit (e.g., alarm at 90 °C if limit is 95 °C).
- Log temperature trend; gradual upward drift may indicate coolant degradation.
- Perform thermal imaging quarterly to detect hot spots in equipment.
