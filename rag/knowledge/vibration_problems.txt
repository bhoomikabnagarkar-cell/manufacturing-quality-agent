VIBRATION PROBLEMS AND CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
==========================================

1. CAUSES OF ELEVATED VIBRATION
---------------------------------
Vibration in manufacturing machines is primarily caused by:
- Bearing wear or damage (most common cause, >40% of cases).
- Rotor or spindle imbalance.
- Misalignment between motor shaft and driven shaft.
- Loosened fasteners or mounting bolts.
- Gear tooth wear or damage.
- Resonance at specific operating speeds.
- Foundation issues (uneven floor, loose anchor bolts).

2. VIBRATION SEVERITY LEVELS
------------------------------
Based on ISO 10816 and common industry practice:

Level            Vibration (mm/s RMS)   Action Required
---------        --------------------   ---------------
Normal           0.5 – 1.5              None
Warning          1.6 – 3.4              Schedule maintenance within 1 week
Critical         3.5 – 5.5              Stop machine; immediate inspection
Danger           > 5.5                  Emergency shutdown; risk of machine damage

3. DEFECTS CAUSED BY EXCESSIVE VIBRATION
------------------------------------------
- Poor surface finish on machined or formed parts.
- Dimensional variation (chatter marks, out-of-round features).
- Tool chipping or breakage during machining.
- Fatigue cracks in structural components over time.
- Loose fasteners in assembled products.
- Inconsistent weld bead geometry in welding operations.

4. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS FOR HIGH VIBRATION
-----------------------------------------
Step 1 – Immediate actions:
  1. Reduce machine speed by 10–20% to check if vibration decreases.
  2. If vibration is Critical, stop the machine immediately.

Step 2 – Diagnosis:
  3. Use a vibration analyzer or simple accelerometer to locate the source.
  4. Check all bearing temperatures by touch or infrared thermometer.
  5. Inspect motor and shaft coupling alignment with a dial indicator.
  6. Check and tighten all mounting bolts (torque to specification).

Step 3 – Repair:
  7. Replace worn or damaged bearings.
  8. Perform dynamic balancing on the rotor/spindle.
  9. Realign motor-to-gearbox or gearbox-to-machine coupling.
  10. Replace worn gears or apply gear lubrication.

Step 4 – Verification:
  11. Restart machine and measure vibration again.
  12. Run at normal speed for 10 minutes; confirm vibration is within normal range.

5. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE FOR VIBRATION
-----------------------------------------
- Lubricate bearings every 500 operating hours (or per manufacturer schedule).
- Check alignment after any motor or gearbox replacement.
- Torque-check all foundation bolts monthly.
- Trend vibration levels; a gradual increase indicates developing wear.
- Replace bearings proactively at 80% of rated service life.
